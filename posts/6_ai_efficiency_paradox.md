In physics, *efficiency* is simply *how much useful output can be produced by a given input*. It can be expressed as a simple formula:

`Efficiency = (Useful Output / Total Input) x 100`

For a knowledge worker such as a software engineer, efficiency might be how quickly you can produce features. Certainly, delegating tasks to an AI can drastically speed up this process and thus increase efficiency.

But we know that our efficiency as engineers also grows with experience. A seasoned engineer can produce a lot more, or better, output than an inexperienced one. Our simple formula does not capture this.

For knowledge workers, a better view of efficiency is one in which your efficiency compounds over time. As you gain familiarity with a task, your ability to execute it amplifies. The very act of producing, results in an increased capacity to produce more or better results down the line.

Delegating everything to AI can boost the first kind of efficiency, but quietly erode the second. We need to be mindful of what we delegate.

## Mindful Delegation

Mindful delegation is simply the practice of being intentional about what you delegate to AI. It's about paying attention to what you delegate and, perhaps more importantly, what you don't.

**Do not delegate what you don't know**
What you do not know today is what could be knowledge tomorrow. Knowledge gained has applicability beyond the immediate ask. You can simply stash it away and retrieve it each time you need it later on. Never delegate an opportunity to learn.

**Do not delegate what is hard**
Doing hard things trains your mind and teaches you persistence and patience. Rare and valuable skills are what count as your capital as a knowledge worker, and acquiring them involves doing hard things. Hand the hard parts to AI and you're handing away the very work that grows your capital.

**Do not delegate all the time**
Over-reliance on AI leads to skill atrophy over time. You simply forget how to do your job. From time to time, employ an abstinence window where you avoid using AI altogether. This may seem "Luddite-ish" at first, but if you think about it, you do this all the time with exercise. Because modern life is so comfortable (and sedentary), you deliberately engage in some kind of artificially induced resistance so that your physical skills do not atrophy. It's the same thing applied to your cognitive skills.

**Do not delegate accountability**
This is true for all kinds of delegation, and certainly true for AI as well.
Using AI to produce something does not absolve you of ownership or accountability. The work that you produce, with or without an AI assistant to take the blame, must be subject to the same level of scrutiny. Anthropomorphising AI tricks your brain into distancing itself from accountability, but it's important to realise that AI is only a tool, and you, as its wielder, hold responsibility for what it produces.

AI is fast becoming ubiquitous in knowledge work. Practising the above rules will help you be efficient today without losing your edge tomorrow.
